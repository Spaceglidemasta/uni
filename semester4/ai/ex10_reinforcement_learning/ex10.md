MDP_a = (T, S, A, p, r, \gamma)

$$

where $t_n$ is the point in time when the pole falls over.

$$
S = \{s_l, s_m, s_r\}
$$

where $s_l$ is the State of the pole beeing to the left, $s_r$ to the right and $s_m$ when the sensors detect the pole beeing perfectly at a 90° angle.

$$
A = \{a_l, a_s, a_r\}
$$

accelerate left, stop, accelerate right. Alternativly, these actions would also take in a value $a$ of type $m/s^2$, representing the acceleration of the action.

$$
p = (i, a, j)
$$

where $i$ the poles current facing direction, $a$ the questioned action, and $p$ the resulting probability of reaching state $j$ with $a$ from $i$.

$$
r = (s, a)
$$

where $s$ is the current pole direction and $a$ the action, so that $r$ is the resulting __reward__ from taking $a$ on $s$. For example, the reward would be negative for accelerating left while the pole is already left tilted.

$$
\gamma \in [0;1]
$$

some reward constant for $r$.

## Exercise 2: Basic Terminology
### a)

The __Markov decision process__ is a 6-Tuple $(T, S, A, p, r, \gamma)$ Representing all possible Time points $T$, all states $S$, all actions $A$, the probability function $p(i,a,j)$ where $i$ is the current state, $a$ is the action to be performed and $p$ is the resulting probability of getting to state $j$ after performing $a$ on $i$, $r(s, a)$ is the reward function returning the reward for a given action $a$ on a state $s$ and $\gamma$ is the discount constant effecting the rate as the reward at which the reward goes down for specific actions in the future. The further away a specific action on a specific state is in the future, the higher the discount. 

The __Model__ is the "world" the $RL$ machine is in (or atleast where the machine thinks its in). Its an abstract enviroment created beforehand, which returns results from a given action on a given state. It may be (partly) given to the machine on initilisation, or not at all. The machine needs to _explore_ the model on its own.

The __Policy__ is the way the machine makes decisions. It decides
which actions make the most sense and if its worth to explore or exploit the model.

A Value  Function returns a rating of how optimal it is to be in a specific position or a performing a specific action on a specific position. It utilizes the Policy, Current State and, depending on the type of the value function, the next action to give out an expected reward.

### b)
The extreme factor setting of $\gamma = 0$ means that every action after the first yields no reward to be calculated. This would mean that the RF machine does not care about any future rewards, only about the next one. 

The extreme factor setting of $\gamma \rightarrow 1$ means that the worth of future rewards decline at an infinitly small rate, practially resulting in the RF taking into account all future rewards, setting them all equaly no mather when they will occur. This equals a non-discounted return.
